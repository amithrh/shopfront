About Thimbleberry

A small bakery with one big oven, on the corner of SE Thimbleberry Street and SE 32nd Avenue since 2011.

From a market table to a corner shop

Rosa Ibáñez learned to bake in her grandmother's kitchen in Tucson, Arizona, rolling tortillas on weeknights and braiding bread for Sunday dinners. After moving to Portland she spent five years baking bread overnight for restaurants, and in 2008 she started selling sourdough from a folding table at a Saturday farmers market.

Three summers later the line was long enough to justify a lease. Thimbleberry Bakehouse opened in 2011 in a former hardware store on SE Thimbleberry Street, and the old shelving still holds our bread today.

We're still small on purpose: one deck oven, one decorating bench and a team of nine people who mostly start work before sunrise.

What we bake with

Our flour is milled in the Willamette Valley, our eggs come from a family farm in Yamhill County, and the hazelnuts in our shortbread and praline grow in orchards south of Salem. Butter is cultured, chocolate is dark, and nothing comes out of a mix.

Whatever is left at closing goes to a neighborhood food pantry that same evening, so nothing we bake is sold the next day.
